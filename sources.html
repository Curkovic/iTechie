AI-Generated Textread with care

Some lines on the homepage are written by an AI model, run through a self-hosted gateway (OmniRoute) on the home server. The models behind it vary. AI text can be wrong, so check the original sources before relying on it.

  • Rewritten from the sources above: asteroid alert, peace index, blackout summary, and the headlines of the day: the AI only translates the four titles and summarises the main story's intro from the feed. It does not choose the stories. Every story links to the original Croatian article.
  • Written from the model's own knowledge: daily knowledge, dead tech (topic area: 1990s in computing), and the apocalypse skill of the week.
  • City Hall: the AI translates the City of Zadar’s official notices into English (only new ones, usually once a day). It does not sort or pick them. If the translation fails, the previous block stays up.
  • The line next to the logo: every 3 hours the AI writes one short comment, in the site's cyberpunk voice, on one of the headlines of the day (they take turns; the AI does not choose). Hover it to see which headline; click it to read the original article.
  • Written on my phone: the go‑outside line in the ticker is written by Apple's AI model from the current weather, via an iPhone Shortcut.
  • Zadar This Morning: every morning an iPhone Shortcut looks at a public live street camera of Five Wells Square and Apple's AI model describes the weather and how busy it is. Only the text is published, never the image.

Hosting & Privacywhat your browser talks to

The site is static and hosted on GitHub Pages. No analytics, no cookies, no ads, no third-party scripts. Everything loads from this domain except the news thumbnails (national and local), the recommendation poster, the NASA image and the On This Day picture (Wikimedia), which your browser loads directly from the news sites, Plex and NASA (without telling it which page you came from). GitHub, as the host, may keep standard server logs; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.

No Banners Heremy take

Why there’s no cookie banner. EU rules (the ePrivacy Directive and the GDPR) say a site must ask before it stores or reads anything on your device that isn’t strictly needed, or before it uses your personal data for things like tracking and advertising. This site does none of that: no cookies, no local storage, no analytics, no fingerprinting, no ads, no third-party scripts. There is nothing to ask you about, so there is no banner.

What I think of those rules. I’m not a fan of how these EU laws turned out in practice. The goal was privacy. What we got is millions of pop-ups nobody reads, “Accept all” buttons designed to be easier than “Reject”, and small sites copy-pasting legal templates while the big platforms carry on almost as before. A banner doesn’t make a site private. Not collecting anything does.

So instead of a banner, I simply don’t track you. If a site needs a pop-up to tell you how many “partners” it shares your visit with, that tells you something too.

The honest small print: GitHub, which hosts the site, keeps standard server logs. A few images (news thumbnails, the recommendation poster, the NASA image) load straight from their own sites, which see your IP address like any image request. Links to other sites follow those sites’ rules. This section is my personal opinion, not legal advice.
