Local NGOs Attend GDPR Education Workshop in Zadar

An educational workshop regarding data protection regulations for local associations was recently held at the Zadar City Library. Fifteen participants gathered to learn about their administrative obligations under the General Data Protection Regulation. The session was led by Nives Rogoznica from the Eko-Zadar association, which organized the event as part of a broader civic participation project. Following the presentation, attendees discussed potential future collaborations and the possibility of receiving one-on-one administrative support. Organizers have prepared a brief online survey to gauge further interest in these support services. This initiative was funded by the Zadar County budget through a public call for civil society projects.

ONE HUNDRED PERCENT, UNTIL THE UPGRADEMiles · 28 Nov 2026

In 2023, the leadership of the US Air Force's Cloud One platform wrote a public commentary boasting a "100% success rate" against a "staggering" number of attempts to break in. "There is no room for error when it comes to security," they added. This column is about that number. On Friday, Reuters reported, citing three people familiar with the matter, that hackers had breached Cloud One, the platform built since 2017 on Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet infrastructure to run Air Force pay, personnel, logistics, air defense command and control, and a system tracking munitions at more than 200 locations. One source said the intruders reached the control plane, which they likened to a universal remote. Nobody has said who did it, or whether anything was taken. The Pentagon replied that its systems are "safe, secure," and declined to discuss the status of its networks. A success rate is a strange thing to measure in defense. It counts the doors that held and has no column for the door nobody noticed was open. The intrusion was found during upgrade work, which means the perfect record was not broken by an attack. It was broken by someone finally looking closely enough to see one. This is not a special failing of generals. Every system that has ever been declared secure was declared secure using the information available to the people who built it. That is the only information anyone has. The error is not in the confidence; it is in printing the confidence as a statistic, as if defense were a scoreboard and not a guess about the future.
