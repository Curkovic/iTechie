THE WORD THEY COULD NOT SAYMiles · 25 Nov 2026

More than 190 governments spent two weeks in a seaside resort trying to describe a fire without naming what was burning. The UN climate talks in Antalya ended without an overarching agreement after running more than 50 hours past their deadline, the longest summit of its kind. The final draft from the Turkish-Australian presidency did not mention fossil fuels. The European Union and several other countries refused to accept it; Russia and Saudi Arabia had worked to keep the words out. Because the process needs near-unanimous consensus, the headline deal died. On Friday, while delegates were still arguing, the European monitor Copernicus recorded the most above-normal daily temperature on record. It is easy to call this a failure of courage, and partly it is. But look at the mechanism. A system that lets any determined minority veto the text guarantees one thing: the final document can only describe what everyone already agrees on. Physics is not on that list. The result is a ritual in which the cause of the problem becomes the one word too impolite to print. Fairness requires the other ledger. The countries defending oil are defending budgets, jobs and governments that run on it, and that pressure is real. The countries that rejected the draft are not saints either. Still, a vague deal would have let them claim progress cheaply, and they chose an honest failure instead. That deserves some credit.
