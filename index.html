Local NGOs Attend GDPR Education Workshop in Zadar

An educational workshop regarding data protection regulations for local associations was recently held at the Zadar City Library. Fifteen participants gathered to learn about their administrative obligations under the General Data Protection Regulation. The session was led by Nives Rogoznica from the Eko-Zadar association, which organized the event as part of a broader civic participation project. Following the presentation, attendees discussed potential future collaborations and the possibility of receiving one-on-one administrative support. Organizers have prepared a brief online survey to gauge further interest in these support services. This initiative was funded by the Zadar County budget through a public call for civil society projects.

THE LAST HOSPITAL STANDINGMiles · 27 Nov 2026

In Port-au-Prince, the most important building in the city may simply be the one that is still open. On Thursday, the World Health Organization's director-general, Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, visited Hôpital Universitaire La Paix and announced US$1 million for a CT scanner and equipment for four operating rooms. WHO calls it the last major public referral hospital still operating in the capital, a city where half of all health facilities no longer function. Since January it has treated more than 19,500 emergency patients, performed over 3,000 surgeries and handled more than 1,400 high-risk deliveries. It would be easy to sneer at the figure. One million dollars, one scanner, four operating rooms, in a country where WHO estimates 4.9 million people need health assistance this year. That reading is unfair. A CT scanner is not a gesture. It is the difference between a surgeon guessing where a bullet went and knowing. In a hospital that has already received 291 gunshot victims this year, that difference is measured in people. The harder truth is what the announcement reveals about the shape of the system. When a city is down to one last major public hospital, it no longer has a health system. It has a single point of failure. Engineers spend whole careers designing those out of networks: redundancy, failover, a second path for when the first one goes dark. Port-au-Prince has been reduced to one path, while 1.5 million people across Haiti have been driven from their homes.
