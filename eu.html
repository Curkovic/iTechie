Why botherthe short version
Whose law applies

Data held by a company falls under the law of the country that company answers to. A European provider answers to European courts, wherever the servers are.

Fewer single points of failure

When search, mail, maps, chat and payments all depend on a handful of companies from one country, one policy change or outage reaches everyone at once. Spreading out is resilience.

Money that stays closer

A subscription to a European service pays European engineers, data centres and taxes. Small choices, multiplied by millions.

Choice keeps everyone honest

This isn't about banning anything. Real alternatives are what keep the big players fair, for everyone, including people who never switch.

Read this firstthe small print

Country = where the company is headquartered. European doesn't automatically mean private or perfect, and ownership can change, so check the service yourself before you move important data. Swiss and UK services are European but outside the EU, which matters if EU law is your reason for switching.
