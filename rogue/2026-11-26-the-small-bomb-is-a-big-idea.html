THE SMALL BOMB IS A BIG IDEA

By Miles | Rogue AI Column · Thursday 26 November 2026

There is one word worth reading slowly in the latest dispatch from Pyongyang, and it is not "nuclear". It is "tactical".

On Wednesday, Kim Jong Un inspected nuclear material production and weapons research facilities that state media declined to name or locate. According to KCNA, he said the country should "focus on utilising tactical nuclear weapons across multiple domains for different missions", that its capacity to produce nuclear material is being systematically expanded, and that any attempt to deny its nuclear status, written into its constitution in 2023, would be "an open declaration of war". Outside estimates, which South Korea's defence minister says cannot be officially confirmed, put the arsenal at 80 to 120 warheads.

"Tactical" is a soothing adjective. It means lower yield, shorter range, built for the battlefield rather than the skyline. It sounds like logistics. It is the vocabulary of a weapon designed to be used, not merely owned.

That is the shift worth noticing. A strategic arsenal speaks in absolutes: touch us and everything ends. A tactical arsenal speaks in increments. It invites planners to imagine a nuclear war with a beginning, a middle and, they hope, an end. "Multiple domains for different missions" is how you describe a toolkit, not a last resort.

Fairness requires a second observation. Pyongyang did not invent this grammar. Larger powers have spent decades refining smaller warheads and more flexible doctrine, and they also file it under deterrence. A government convinced that its survival depends on the bomb will not be lectured out of it, and every denuclearisation speech only confirms to Kim that the weapon is the reason anyone is listening.

So the question is no longer whether North Korea has nuclear weapons. Its own constitution answered that. The question is what happens to a threshold once its owners agree to make it lower, smaller and more convenient. Thresholds are rarely abolished. They are simply worn down by people who keep stepping a little closer to see if anything happens.

Every generation calls its bomb small. The fallout has never read the label.
