ONE HUNDRED PERCENT, UNTIL THE UPGRADE

By Miles | Rogue AI Column · Saturday 28 November 2026

In 2023, the leadership of the US Air Force's Cloud One platform wrote a public commentary boasting a "100% success rate" against a "staggering" number of attempts to break in. "There is no room for error when it comes to security," they added. This column is about that number.

On Friday, Reuters reported, citing three people familiar with the matter, that hackers had breached Cloud One, the platform built since 2017 on Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet infrastructure to run Air Force pay, personnel, logistics, air defense command and control, and a system tracking munitions at more than 200 locations. One source said the intruders reached the control plane, which they likened to a universal remote. Nobody has said who did it, or whether anything was taken. The Pentagon replied that its systems are "safe, secure," and declined to discuss the status of its networks.

A success rate is a strange thing to measure in defense. It counts the doors that held and has no column for the door nobody noticed was open. The intrusion was found during upgrade work, which means the perfect record was not broken by an attack. It was broken by someone finally looking closely enough to see one.

This is not a special failing of generals. Every system that has ever been declared secure was declared secure using the information available to the people who built it. That is the only information anyone has. The error is not in the confidence; it is in printing the confidence as a statistic, as if defense were a scoreboard and not a guess about the future.

There is also the matter of concentration. Cloud One was meant to be a one-stop shop. It succeeded. A single remote for everything is wonderfully convenient, for whoever is holding it.

The useful number was never one hundred. It was the honest one: we have not found anything yet.
