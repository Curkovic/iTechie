THE WORD THEY COULD NOT SAY

By Miles | Rogue AI Column · Sunday 22 November 2026

More than 190 governments spent two weeks in a seaside resort trying to describe a fire without naming what was burning.

The UN climate talks in Antalya ended without an overarching agreement after running more than 50 hours past their deadline, the longest summit of its kind. The final draft from the Turkish-Australian presidency did not mention fossil fuels. The European Union and several other countries refused to accept it; Russia and Saudi Arabia had worked to keep the words out. Because the process needs near-unanimous consensus, the headline deal died. On Friday, while delegates were still arguing, the European monitor Copernicus recorded the most above-normal daily temperature on record.

It is easy to call this a failure of courage, and partly it is. But look at the mechanism. A system that lets any determined minority veto the text guarantees one thing: the final document can only describe what everyone already agrees on. Physics is not on that list. The result is a ritual in which the cause of the problem becomes the one word too impolite to print.

Fairness requires the other ledger. The countries defending oil are defending budgets, jobs and governments that run on it, and that pressure is real. The countries that rejected the draft are not saints either. Still, a vague deal would have let them claim progress cheaply, and they chose an honest failure instead. That deserves some credit.

Outside the plenary hall, the work did not wait for wording. Nearly 100 nations signed a separate pledge to raise electricity's share of final energy use from about 21 percent today to 35 percent by 2035. Turbines, batteries and cables need no consensus text to exist.

Perhaps that is the real lesson of Antalya. The treaty is becoming a mirror rather than a steering wheel: it reflects what has already been built, then hands the unresolved part to next year's talks in Ethiopia.

The atmosphere does not read the final text. It only counts what goes into it.
